Visual signature vs. certificate-based digital signature

PDFora places a visual signature that you draw or type onto a PDF page. This can be suitable for informal approvals or workflows where the recipient accepts a visible signature. It is not the same as a cryptographic digital signature backed by a certificate, identity verification, timestamping, or an electronic-signature audit trail.

Placement and document review

A signature can be visually correct but still be placed on the wrong page or cover nearby text. Before exporting, confirm the selected page, signature size, and position. After download, reopen the PDF and verify the signature is visible at normal zoom and that no important content was obscured.

Privacy and file handling

The selected PDF and signature drawing are processed in the browser for this workflow. PDFora creates a new output file instead of changing your original. Keep the original document until you know the signed copy is accepted for the intended use.

When another signing method is required

Some organizations require identity verification, a certificate-backed digital signature, a timestamp, an audit trail, or a specific e-signature platform. A visual signature placed by this tool does not provide those additional controls. Follow the receiving organization’s stated signing requirements for official or regulated documents.

A visual signature is not the same as a certificate-based digital signature

Drawing or typing a signature places a visible mark on the page. That can be useful for informal approvals and workflows where the recipient accepts an image-style signature. It does not automatically create a cryptographic digital signature, identity certificate, tamper-evident validation chain, or legally verified e-signature service.

Before using a visual signature on a contract, government form, regulated filing, or high-value transaction, confirm what the recipient requires. Some organizations accept a placed signature image; others require an approved electronic-signature platform or certificate-based signing method.

Place and verify the signature carefully

Check the page number, signature line, scale, and surrounding text before export. Open the saved PDF again after downloading and make sure the signature remains visible. Do not treat a visual mark as proof that another person signed the document unless your workflow separately establishes identity and authorization.

Keep evidence requirements separate from appearance

A signature that looks correct on the page answers a visual question, not necessarily an identity or audit question. If a process requires evidence of who signed, when they signed, consent, or document integrity, use a signing method that records the evidence required by that process.

For informal workflows, the visible signature may be sufficient, but the recipient—not the appearance of the mark—determines what is acceptable.

Use a descriptive final filename

After placing the signature, save the result with a filename that distinguishes it from the unsigned source. Open the saved copy once more before sending it, especially when several versions of the same form are stored in the same folder.
